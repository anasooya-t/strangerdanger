I Want to Talk to Strangers

It was 11:30 am when Adeen Asad Mirza opened her iPad and decided to talk to online strangers from her bedroom in Dubai. Mirza who is now an 18-year-old woman preparing to go to college used to speak with anonymous strangers online with her friends since she was 10-years-old through websites like umingle.com...

“Tons of weird experiences. I can write you an essay on the shit I have seen,” said Mirza as she talked with me on the website.

Often known by different names these roulette-styled chatting platforms allowed users to speak with strangers of any age without an account. Mirza had first found out about such websites from her friends from school who were already using them even before they had their own phones.

The number of random video chatting websites on the internet like umingle.com that provides services where children can openly connect and speak with adult strangers without any oversight or regulation has increased. These platforms that favors the anonymity of the user, makes it easy for people to connect with strangers and have conversations about any subject.

These websites that are often known for having explicit online sexual conversations and cyberflashing are also frequented by children who can easily access them without restrictions. Child protection agencies and online child safety groups around the world have been continuously pushing awareness about these online spaces as many such websites were reported on for connecting sexual predators with children. But as there are large number of websites that work on the same principles, identifying online harm against children on these platforms have become increasingly difficult.

Omegle.com was the first few websites that popularised the idea of talking to online strangers. Developed by Leif K. Brooks an 18-years-old software programmer in Oregon, the website used to get upto 66 million monthly visits and was a cultural moment as social media influencers and YouTubers began streaming content from the conversations they have, making the space even more popular.

“If the Internet is a manifestation of the “global village”, Omegle was meant to be a way of strolling down a street in that village, striking up conversations with the people you ran into along the way,” Brooks said on his website that shut down in 2023 due to numerous lawsuits.

The National Center for Missing and Exploited Children that records reports of online exploitation of children, received 6,08,601 reports against Omegle in 2022 alone. The website that gained even more popularity during the pandemic was finally declared bankrupt and was shutdown by Brooks after a case where a 11-year-old was paired on the website with a man in his late 30’s who groomed the child for three years.

“In some ways, what we feared has happened - a Hydra effect, where closing a popular webcam platform like Omegle leads to the problem becoming more diffuse, and harder to contain,” said Paul Bleakley a researcher at the Middlesex University in London, who conducted a study about adult users who had sexual interactions with children on these chatrooms.

Teenagers like Mirza who keeps coming back to use such websites out of boredom, shares how easy it is to find people flashing themselves, masterbating or saying racist and sexist slurs as they can stay anonymous.

According to Naomi Leeds, an attorney from Brooklyn who represented the case against Omegle, one of the major legal difficulty of dealing with online child sexual abuse in websites like Omegle were that these spaces did not hold liability until a large enough harm took place on the platform.
